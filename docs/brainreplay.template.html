What you're looking at

The eye

The game tells the brain how fast the next spike is growing in view. That's what "looming" means: something getting bigger fast is about to hit you. Far away the alarm cells (orange, green) stay almost quiet. Up close they fire hard.

The wiring

Nothing in our code says "jump now". The alarm spreads through the real FlyWire wiring, waking up a few hundred neurons (blue) along the way. If enough of it reaches the Giant Fiber, the fly's escape neuron, the Giant Fiber fires. That spike is the button press.

The scrambled brain

Switch to Scrambled wiring. It has the same neurons and the same number of connections per neuron, but who connects to whom is shuffled at random. The alarm cells fire just the same, yet the signal never reaches the jump cell, and the square runs straight into the first spike.

Recorded run, played back. The model takes about as long as the fly would to work through each 1/60 s frame (the game waits for it), so we record every spike once and replay it at any speed. 100% of the level is the brain's own decision; the only numbers we set are how the alarm grows with looming (r_ref, steep).

Connectome-constrained leaky integrate-and-fire model (Shiu et al. 2024) on FlyWire FAFB v783 (Dorkenwald et al. 2024; Schlegel et al. 2024). Data CC BY-NC 4.0. Positions are FlyWire soma locations where known, otherwise a point on the neuron.
